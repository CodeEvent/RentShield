## The rule

Article 25(3) of Law No. (33) of 2008 requires a landlord's notice to be
served through one of a limited set of methods for it to be legally
effective. Serving notice by an unlisted method (e.g. WhatsApp, a plain
unregistered email, or simply telling the tenant verbally) does not
satisfy the statutory requirement, however clearly it communicates intent.

## Recognized methods

1. **Notary Public** (Kاتب العدل) — the most common and most defensible
   method; produces an official notarized record of service.
2. **Registered mail** with acknowledgment of receipt, sent to the
   tenant's address as registered on the Ejari contract.
3. **Court bailiff** (محضر) service — used when the landlord anticipates
   the tenant may dispute receipt, or is already engaging the courts.

## Practical guidance

- Use the address on file with Ejari, not an address the tenant has
  informally moved to, unless a contract addendum updates it.
- Keep the notarized certificate / registered-mail receipt / bailiff
  report — RDSC filings (see `rdsc-filing-uae`) require proof of valid
  service as a threshold matter; a case can be dismissed or delayed
  without it.
- If the tenant is unreachable or actively evading service, a court
  bailiff's attempted-service report is generally the strongest evidence
  of good-faith compliance.
