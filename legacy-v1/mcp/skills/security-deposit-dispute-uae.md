## Background

Dubai tenancy contracts typically require a refundable security deposit
(commonly 5% of annual rent for unfurnished units, higher for furnished),
held by the landlord or a managing agent, and returnable at the end of
the tenancy less any lawful deductions.

## Common lawful deductions

- Unpaid rent or service charges outstanding at handover
- Damage beyond normal wear and tear, evidenced by a check-in/check-out
  condition report or dated photographs
- Cleaning costs, only if the contract specifies a return-condition
  standard the tenant failed to meet

## What is generally NOT a lawful deduction

- Normal wear and tear (fading paint, minor carpet wear, etc.)
- Costs unrelated to damage the tenant caused (e.g. pre-existing defects)
- An amount not itemized or evidenced

## If the deposit isn't returned within a reasonable period

1. Request an itemized statement of deductions in writing.
2. If unresolved, the dispute can be filed with the RDSC alongside, or
   independently of, any eviction proceedings (see
   `rdsc-filing-uae`) — deposit disputes do not require an eviction
   notice to have been served first.
3. Evidence that materially strengthens a claim: the signed tenancy
   contract, the check-in condition report/photos, and any move-out
   condition report/photos.

## What this skill does NOT do

It does not calculate a specific refund amount or draft a demand letter —
that requires the actual contract terms and condition-report evidence,
which this platform does not currently collect.
