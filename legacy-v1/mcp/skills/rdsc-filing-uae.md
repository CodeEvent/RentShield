## When this applies

The tenant did not comply with a validly served notice (see
`draft_eviction_notice` / `calculate_notice_expiry`) by its expiry or
compliance date, and the landlord wants to pursue eviction or recovery of
amounts due through Dubai's Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC).

## Typical steps

1. **Confirm the notice was valid and its period has expired.** A case
   filed before the statutory period (30 or 365 days) has run is
   generally premature.
2. **Gather documents**: Ejari certificate/contract, proof the notice was
   served through a valid method (Notary Public, registered mail, or
   court bailiff — see `check_notice_service_method_validity`), and any
   supporting evidence of the statutory ground relied upon (sale
   contract, personal-use declaration, demolition permit, etc.).
3. **File the claim** with the RDSC (part of the Dubai Land Department),
   in person or via the Dubai courts' e-services, specifying the relief
   sought (eviction, rent arrears, damages).
4. **Pay the filing fee** — RDSC fees are calculated against the claim
   value; confirm the current schedule directly with the RDSC rather than
   assuming a fixed figure.
5. **Attend the hearing(s).** RDSC matters typically move faster than
   ordinary civil courts but still involve at least one hearing before a
   judgment.
6. **Enforcement.** A judgment in the landlord's favor may still require
   a separate enforcement request if the tenant does not vacate or pay
   voluntarily.

## What this skill does NOT do

It does not calculate fees, does not generate the RDSC claim form, and
does not track case status — those require direct interaction with the
RDSC/Dubai Courts systems, which are outside this platform's scope today.
